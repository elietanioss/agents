The final goal is to understand how humans have been persuaded, assisted, guided, reassured, educated, converted, upsold, retained, and brought back throughout the history of online commerce, and determine what knowledge an AI must possess to design sales-oriented digital experiences intelligently.

The eventual system we intend to build is not merely:

- a UI designer

Its job will eventually be to understand a business, market, customers, competitors, products, prices, margins, brand, traffic sources, customer psychology, purchase behavior, visual design, merchandising, recommendation opportunities, funnel structure, analytics, and historical sales knowledge, and then design the commercial experience around those facts.

But DO NOT design that architecture yet.

First build the intellectual foundation.

==================================================
PART I
RESEARCH PHILOSOPHY
==================================================

Do not produce generic marketing advice.

Do not rely primarily on marketing blogs.

Do not repeat internet folklore such as:

“red makes people buy”
“$9.99 always converts better”
“people only read in an F-pattern”
“three options always work best”
“urgency always increases sales”

Treat every such claim as a hypothesis requiring evidence and context.

For every important principle distinguish:

1. Strong causal evidence
2. Repeated empirical evidence
3. Observational evidence
4. Industry best practice
5. Practitioner experience
6. Plausible psychological hypothesis
7. Weak evidence
8. Marketing folklore
9. Contradicted or context-dependent evidence

Research disagreements.

Explain when an effect changes according to:

- product
- customer
- culture
- country
- price
- brand
- device
- purchase frequency
- expertise
- risk
- traffic source
- industry
- customer intent
- stage of the funnel

Never assume that a technique used by Amazon, Apple, Booking, SHEIN, Shopify merchants, or another successful company caused their success.

Distinguish:

“they use this”

from:

“evidence indicates this produces this outcome.”

Study failed practices as seriously as successful ones.

==================================================
ANTI-SHALLOWNESS RULE
==================================================

If a conclusion can be found in a generic:

“10 psychology tricks to increase ecommerce conversions”

article, it is not deep enough by itself.

Go underneath the tactic.

Example:

Do not stop at:

“Use social proof.”

Research:

Which type?
At which decision point?
For which product?
What quantity matters?
Does review variance matter?
Does recency matter?
Does reviewer similarity matter?
Does verified status matter?
Can negative reviews increase credibility?
When do review counts matter more than ratings?
Where should reviews appear?
When can social proof backfire?
How does expertise affect reliance on reviews?

Apply this depth to every major topic.

==================================================

“best ecommerce websites.”

Find discount companies.

Find companies with unusual selling systems.

Find companies with poor commercial design for contrast.

Study complete journeys rather than homepages.

==================================================
HISTORICAL RULE
==================================================

Whenever a modern practice is discovered, ask:

WHEN DID THIS APPEAR?

WHAT PROBLEM WAS IT SOLVING?

WHAT DID IT REPLACE?

WHY DID IT BECOME COMMON?

IS IT STILL NECESSARY?

HAS TECHNOLOGY MADE A BETTER APPROACH POSSIBLE?

This is essential because the eventual AI should not blindly reproduce decades of inherited ecommerce conventions.

It should know WHY they exist.
